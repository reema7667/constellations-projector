# Catalogue attribution and teaching-shape notes

The records in `stars.ts` use ICRS/J2000 right ascension and declination and Johnson V apparent magnitude. Star identities and proper-name spellings were checked against the [IAU Working Group on Star Names](https://www.iau.org/public/themes/naming_stars/) and individual [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/) object records (accessed 2026-08-05). SIMBAD is a dynamic meta-compilation, not a homogeneous catalogue; CDS asks users to acknowledge SIMBAD and cite the original catalogues. SIMBAD data are available under the Open Database Licence.

The Scorpius queries were Pi Sco, Beta1 Sco (Acrab), Delta Sco (Dschubba), Alpha Sco (Antares), Tau Sco, Epsilon Sco, Mu1 Sco, Zeta2 Sco, Eta Sco, Theta Sco (Sargas), Kappa Sco, Lambda Sco (Shaula), and Upsilon Sco (Lesath). The Sagittarius queries were Gamma2 Sgr (Alnasl), Delta Sgr (Kaus Media), Lambda Sgr (Kaus Borealis), Epsilon Sgr (Kaus Australis), Zeta Sgr (Ascella), Tau Sgr, Sigma Sgr (Nunki), and Phi Sgr.

The IAU defines constellation names and boundaries, but it does not prescribe these stick figures. Every edge in `constellations.ts` is a locally authored pedagogical choice, not copied from a third-party line catalogue:

- Scorpius joins its three-star head to Antares, then follows the bright southern curve through the tail to the close Shaula–Lesath stinger. Antares anchors the label.
- Sagittarius uses the familiar Teapot as a learning aid: Alnasl forms the spout, Kaus Media/Kaus Borealis/Phi/Nunki/Tau/Ascella/Kaus Australis form the vessel, and Kaus Australis anchors the label. The Teapot is an asterism within Sagittarius, not an IAU constellation.

These deliberately small member lists describe the displayed learning silhouettes only; they are not exhaustive boundary membership lists.
